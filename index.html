Why Plant Cacao? 🍫🌲

🌍 Cacao Helps Save the Forest

Cacao trees grow best under the shade of taller trees. This means when we plant cacao, we don't cut down the forest — we actually protect it! Cacao agroforestry is a method of growing cacao alongside other trees, creating a diverse ecosystem that benefits both people and nature.


By growing cacao, farmers can earn a living without destroying the forest. The cacao trees provide fruit and income, while the surrounding trees continue to produce oxygen, protect wildlife, and keep our soil healthy.

🌳

Protects Trees

Cacao grows in shade — so we keep our existing forests standing while still producing food and income.

💰

Supports Farmers

Farmers earn steady income from cacao beans, making forest protection profitable for local communities.

🦜

Helps Wildlife

Shade-grown cacao preserves habitats for birds, insects, and forest animals that would otherwise lose their homes.

🌤️

Fights Climate Change

More trees mean more carbon is absorbed from the air — helping cool our planet naturally.

How It Works 📋

🌱 From Seed to Harvest

1

Choose the Right Location

Select land that was previously cleared but still has some trees. Cacao needs shade, warm temperatures, and well-drained soil.

2

Prepare the Seeds

Choose healthy, ripe cacao pods. Extract the beans, clean them, and let them dry slightly before planting.

3

Plant with Care

Plant seeds or seedlings under the shade of larger trees. Space them properly so each tree has room to grow without competing for sunlight.

4

Nurture & Grow

Water young trees regularly. Add organic compost to keep the soil healthy. Keep the area free of weeds but don't remove the shade trees!

5

Harvest & Share

In 3–5 years, harvest your first cacao pods! Sell the beans or make chocolate. Use the income to plant even more trees!
